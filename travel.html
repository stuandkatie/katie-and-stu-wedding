Getting Here

We're getting married at the AMERON Neuschwanstein Alpsee Resort & Spa, Alpseestraße 21, 87645 Schwangau, Germany, in the tiny village of Hohenschwangau below the Neuschwanstein and Hohenschwangau castles.

Where to Stay

We've reserved a block of rooms at the AMERON, which is where the wedding itself is happening. Everything for the weekend is within walking distance of your room, so staying here means no transportation to sort out and no reason to leave the party early. You can see the rooms and the property at ameroncollection.com.

Please book by May 30, 2027. Our block is held only until then. After that date any unclaimed rooms go back to the hotel's general inventory, and Hohenschwangau fills up well in advance.

The good news: each reservation can be cancelled free of charge up to 7 days before arrival.

Rooms and rates

Rates are per room, per night, and include an extensive breakfast buffet, free use of the 4 Elements spa and fitness area (the indoor pool looks out at Hohenschwangau Castle, so pack a swimsuit), a bathrobe, WiFi, bottled water, an in-room coffee machine, and a room safe. A note on the Jägerhaus

The resort was built around three historic houses, and the Jägerhaus is one of them. It sits directly across from the main lobby, so it's a walk across the courtyard rather than a hike, and you'll still use the main buildings for breakfast, the spa, and the restaurants.

It's the charming option: antique furnishings, canopied beds, old-fashioned room keys instead of key cards. It's also the older option, so the rooms are more characterful than sleek, and there's no air conditioning in that building. September nights in the Alps are cool, so it's unlikely to matter, but we'd rather you know before you book than after you arrive.

Step 1: Fly to Munich

Munich Airport (MUC) is the gateway, about two hours from us. There are no nonstops from most U.S. cities, so plan on one connection, usually through Atlanta, Newark, Chicago, or a European hub like Amsterdam, Frankfurt, or Paris.

A few alternates worth pricing out:

  • Memmingen (FMM): closer to us (about 85 km) but served mostly by budget European carriers. Only useful if you're already in Europe.
  • Innsbruck (INN) and Zurich (ZRH): both roughly two to two and a half hours away by car.

Give yourself a buffer. Jet lag plus a two-hour transfer is a lot on arrival day, and we'd love for you to come in a day early.

Step 2: Munich Airport to Hohenschwangau

By train (our recommendation)

About three to three and a half hours, three legs, and it costs around €30 or less. German trains are clean, comfortable, and run on time. Download the DB Navigator app before you leave home. It handles tickets, platforms, and live updates in English.

Leg 1: Airport to Munich Hauptbahnhof (main station) Take the S1 or S8 commuter train from directly beneath the terminal. About 45 minutes, departing every 20 minutes or so.
Leg 2: Munich Hauptbahnhof to Füssen Regional train, about two hours, running roughly hourly. Some departures are direct; others involve a simple cross-platform change at Buchloe. The Füssen trains leave from tracks 27 to 36, which are a long walk from the main concourse, so give yourself 20 minutes to find your platform.
Leg 3: Füssen station to the AMERON Two ways to cover the last two and a half miles:
  • By bus. Step outside the station and catch bus 73 (toward Steingaden / Garmisch-Partenkirchen) or bus 78 (toward Schwangau). Get off at Hohenschwangau / Alpseestraße, sometimes announced as the Neuschwanstein Castles stop. A 10 to 15 minute ride, then about a five-minute walk to the hotel. Departures are timed to follow the train arrivals, so you shouldn't wait long.
  • By taxi. Taxis queue outside the station's main entrance. About five minutes to the hotel door for roughly €15 to €20, worth it if you're arriving late, hauling luggage, or the bus is packed. If none are waiting, the hotel front desk can send a car.

Tickets. Two good options:

  • Deutschland-Ticket: a monthly pass valid on all regional trains and local buses nationwide, at any hour. Best if you're traveling around Germany.
  • Bayern-Ticket: covers up to five people on regional trains and buses throughout Bavaria for one flat fare. The catch: on weekdays it's only valid from 9:00 a.m. onward. Great value if you're arriving as a group.

Two things to know. The 73 and 78 buses get very crowded in the summer months, so leave a little buffer if you're connecting tightly. And Deutsche Bahn runs frequent track maintenance on the Munich to Buchloe stretch, so check bahn.de the morning you travel and you won't be surprised by a replacement bus.

By car

About two hours from the airport. Take the A96 toward the Steingaden/Füssen exit, then the B17 into Füssen, then follow signs for Zu den Königsschlössern (to the royal castles). Roughly five minutes later you're in Hohenschwangau, on Alpseestraße.

⚠️ Please read this before you rent a car

Parking at the AMERON is limited and it is not free. The hotel's guest lot sits across the road from the resort, next to the public lot P4, and runs about €14 per 24 hours. There are only so many spaces, and they are not guaranteed with your room.

Hohenschwangau is one of the most visited villages in Germany. The public lots fill with castle day-trippers by mid-morning, every single day, and there is no overflow to fall back on.

If you do drive: pull up to the hotel entrance first to unload your bags, then collect your parking ticket at check-in and move the car. And please let us know in advance so we can flag it with the hotel.

Honestly, unless you're building a longer Bavaria road trip around the wedding, take the train. You won't need a car once you're here.

By private transfer

Around two hours door to door, and expect a few hundred euros. The hotel's front desk can arrange a car if you contact them ahead of your stay. This is the easy button if you're arriving jet-lagged with a lot of luggage, traveling with kids, or have mobility considerations.
